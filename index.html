C Compute

Data centers that start with the power plan.

New computing capacity is held back by electricity and cooling. We design the hall around both from the first drawing, and put solar and storage on site where the land allows.

Typical hall

Rack power
10 to 120 kW per rack
Cooling
Air, rear-door or direct-to-chip liquid
Redundancy
N+1 power and cooling
Efficiency target
PUE 1.2 or better

  • Ready for AI hardware

    Floors, power and piping sized for dense racks. Liquid cooling loops are designed in, even if you start on air.

  • Modular growth

    Prefabricated power and cooling blocks let you add capacity as demand grows.

  • Efficient by design

    Free cooling where the climate allows, and connections ready for reusing waste heat.

  • Edge and micro sites

    Containerized data centers for factories, campuses and telecom sites.

R Robotics

Robots that earn their floor space.

We put robot arms, cobots and autonomous machines to work on production lines and energy sites. You get a cell that runs, and a team trained to run it.

  • Robotic work cells

  • Cobots for small teams

    Collaborative arms that share a bench with people and can be retaught in an afternoon.

  • Vision and inspection

  • Robots for energy sites

  • Controls and integration

    PLC, SCADA and factory software links, so each cell reports to the rest of the plant.

Six tracks into work that is growing

Pick a track to see what you learn and where it leads.

Solar technician

Install, wire and commission solar arrays, from a rooftop to a 100-hectare farm.

You learn
  • Mounting systems and trackers
  • DC wiring, combiner boxes and inverters
  • Testing with I-V curve tracers and thermal cameras
  • Working safely at height and around live DC
It leads to

Solar installer, operations and maintenance technician, commissioning assistant.

Format

12 weeks. Workshop labs, then placement on a live array.

Battery storage technician

Commission and maintain the grid-scale batteries that make solar useful after dark.

You learn
  • Battery chemistry and battery management systems
  • Power conversion and protection
  • Thermal management and fire safety
  • High-voltage switching procedures
It leads to

Storage technician, commissioning technician, field service technician.

Format

10 weeks. Workshop labs, then placement in a battery yard.

Data center technician

Keep power, cooling and hardware running in a hall that is never allowed to stop.

You learn
  • Power distribution, UPS systems and generators
  • Air and liquid cooling systems
  • Fiber and structured cabling
  • Server hardware swaps and monitoring tools
It leads to

Data center technician, critical facilities technician, cabling technician.

Format

14 weeks. Training hall labs, then shifts in a live facility.

Robotics and automation technician

Set up, program and maintain the robot cells that modern factories depend on.

You learn
  • Programming a robot with a teach pendant
  • PLCs, sensors and machine vision
  • Pneumatics, grippers and end-of-arm tooling
  • Safety systems and preventive maintenance
It leads to

Robot technician, automation technician, maintenance technician.

Format

16 weeks. Robot lab time every week, then placement on a production line.

EV charging technician

Install and service the chargers going into parking lots, depots and highway stops.

  • Electrical safety and inspection

EV charger installer, field service technician.

8 weeks. Workshop labs, then supervised installs.

What future-proof means here.

Technology will change faster than concrete. We build so the site can change with it.

  • Modular

    Sites grow in blocks. Add megawatts, racks or robot cells without rebuilding what is there.

  • Open standards

    Equipment and software speak open protocols, so any part can be swapped for a better one later.

  • Room to upgrade

    Spare conduit, floor strength and cooling headroom for hardware that doesn't exist yet.

  • Local skills

    We hire and train close to the site, so the knowledge stays when the build crew leaves.

  • Measured

    Every site reports its output, efficiency and uptime, so decisions rest on data.

  • Safe from the first drawing

    Safety systems are part of the design. They are never added at the end.

Questions people ask first.

How much land does a solar farm need?

A useful rule of thumb is about 2 hectares (5 acres) for each megawatt of a ground-mounted array. Shape, slope and local rules change the figure, and a site study gives you the real one.

Can solar run a data center around the clock?

Solar with battery storage can cover a large share of the load. The grid or backup generation covers the rest. We size the mix for your site and the uptime you need.

How long does a project take?

A small array or a single robot cell can be running within months. Large solar farms and data centers take longer, mostly because of permits and grid connections. The Assess stage gives you a timeline you can plan around.

Do you work on sites that already exist?

Yes. We add storage to existing solar, liquid cooling to existing halls and robots to existing lines.

Do I need experience to join the Academy?

No. The foundation modules start from the basics. If you already hold an electrical or mechanical trade, you can move through them faster.

How are projects paid for?

Some clients buy the plant outright. Others lease it, or sign a power purchase agreement and pay only for the energy. We help you compare the options before you commit.
